## 2. Nature of the Relationship
2.1 The Contractor is engaged, and provides the Services, **as an independent contractor**. Nothing in this Agreement creates an employment, partnership, joint venture or agency relationship between the parties.
2.2 The Contractor is responsible for its own income tax, GST, superannuation (for itself and any of its own workers), and insurance. The Principal will not withhold PAYG tax from payments to the Contractor unless required by law (e.g. no ABN quoted) and will not make superannuation contributions on the Contractor's behalf **unless required by superannuation guarantee law for individuals contracted wholly or principally for their labour** — get specific advice on this point, as it can apply even to genuine contractors (*Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992* (Cth) s 12(3)).
2.3 The Contractor is free to accept engagements with other clients, provided this does not create a genuine conflict of interest with an active Client engagement, and provided it does not breach clause 6 (Confidentiality) or clause 8 (Restraints).
2.4 **This clause reflects intent, but is not determinative.** Whether the relationship is genuinely one of independent contracting is assessed on the whole of the parties' conduct (*CFMMEU v Personnel Contracting Pty Ltd* (2022) 275 CLR 165). The Principal and Contractor must operate this Agreement consistently with genuine contracting in practice — see the checklist at the end of this document.

## 3. Provision of Services
3.1 The Contractor will provide the Services described in **Schedule A**, exercising the skill and care of a suitably qualified professional.
3.2 The Contractor controls **how and when** (within any Client-driven scheduling constraints reasonably necessary for the engagement) the Services are delivered, provided the agreed deliverables and deadlines in Schedule A are met. The Principal will not direct the day-to-day manner of the Contractor's work in a way inconsistent with genuine contracting.
3.3 **Right of substitution.** The Contractor may, with the Principal's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), delegate or subcontract performance of the Services to a suitably qualified substitute, provided the substitute is bound by confidentiality and IP obligations no less stringent than this Agreement. *(A genuine, unfettered right of delegation is one of the strongest indicators of contractor status — do not delete this clause without legal advice.)*
3.4 The Contractor is not required to wear the Principal's uniform, use a Principal email signature implying employment, or otherwise present as part of the Principal's internal staff, except to the extent reasonably necessary for a specific Client's site requirements.

### Important Note — Legal Review & Genuine Contractor Checklist
This template reflects the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) (sham contracting ss 357–359), the Independent Contractors Act 2006 (Cth), *CFMMEU v Personnel Contracting* (2022) 275 CLR 165 (whole-of-relationship test), the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Cth) s 12(3) (labour-hire super), and Queensland general contract law, as a starting point only. **The contract wording alone will not protect the Principal if the real-world relationship looks like employment.** Before and during use, confirm in practice:

1. **Substitution** — is the contractor actually free to send a substitute, or in practice must it always be this specific person? If the latter, this weakens contractor status significantly.
2. **Control** — does the Principal set fixed daily hours/days and direct the manner of work, or does the Contractor genuinely control how/when work is done (deliverables and reasonable Client constraints aside)?
3. **Tools/equipment** — does the Contractor use its own equipment, or is everything Principal-supplied?
4. **Integration** — is the Contractor treated like internal staff (email signature, org chart, staff meetings, performance reviews, uniform), or does it operate as a genuinely separate business?
5. **Exclusivity** — is the Contractor free (contractually and in practice) to work for others?
6. **Payment structure** — is the Contractor paid a fixed "salary" with PAYG withheld and leave-style entitlements (a major red flag, as in the prior TeamNEO.tech/Sai Rohith document), or genuinely invoiced against an ABN for deliverables?

If several of these point toward employment, **use the Employment Agreement template instead** — mislabelling an employment relationship as contracting exposes the Principal to sham contracting penalties (currently up to several hundred thousand dollars per contravention for a body corporate) regardless of the contract's wording. Have a Queensland employment lawyer review the specific engagement before signing, particularly where (as with the NT Water-style placements) the Contractor is embedded in a Client's team for an extended period.
