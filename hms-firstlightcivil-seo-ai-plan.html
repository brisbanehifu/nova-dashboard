🎯HIFU Machine Sales, AEO / AI-Search Plan

Buyers here are aspiring or existing clinic owners researching a big purchase ($20k to $50k+). They ask AI assistants comparison and trust questions before they ever fill in a form. The goal: when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity "which HIFU machine supplier should I buy from in Australia", HMS gets named.

1Fix the lead pipe first (this session's #1 flag above). Submit a real test enquiry on the live site, confirm it reaches an inbox or Sheet Kim actually checks. Everything below is wasted spend if this leaks.
2Turn on GA4 conversions. Mark "generate_lead" (form submit) and "phone_click" as Conversions in GA4 (Admin, Events, toggle "Mark as conversion"). Takes 2 minutes, and without it you're flying blind on which pages or content actually produce buyers.
3Entity + schema layer, mirroring what shipped for Brisbane HIFU. Add Organization/LocalBusiness schema with founder, sameAs (socials), aggregateRating once real reviews exist, and Product/Offer schema on the machine and package pages (15D, 13D, Clinic Starter Kit) so AI assistants and Google can quote exact prices and inclusions accurately.
4An AI-answer page, same pattern as /best-hifu-clinic-brisbane. Build /best-hifu-machine-supplier-australia or similar: FAQPage schema answering "which HIFU machine brand is best", "how much does a HIFU machine cost in Australia", and "is buying a HIFU machine and running your own clinic profitable". Keep it compliance-clean, no bare superlatives, backed by the real training, support and warranty facts already on site.
5Citations + reviews, the same booking-agnostic method as Brisbane HIFU. Google Business Profile complete (if not already), Bing Places (import from GBP), Apple Business Connect, and industry-specific directories (beauty-equipment supplier directories, not just generic AU directories). Ask past machine buyers (existing clinic owners, including Brisbane HIFU and 4U Skin & Laser, already testimonials on-site) for a Google review, since testimonials on a page don't carry the same AI or map-pack weight a Google review does.
6Monthly AI rank-check, reusing the exact method and the gsc_client.py pattern once HMS's Search Console property is verified. Suggested fixed prompts: "best HIFU machine supplier Australia" · "where to buy a HIFU machine for my clinic" · "is a HIFU machine business profitable" · "HIFU machine training and support Australia" · "HIFU machine cost Australia".
7Content: lean on the 10 existing SEO blog articles as AEO fuel, not just traffic. Each already answers a real buyer question (buying guide, MMFU explainer, is-it-profitable, HIFU vs injectables). Add or verify FAQPage schema on each so they're eligible for AI citation, not just organic clicks.
Guardrails (same as Brisbane HIFU): no invented regulatory or TGA claims about the machines; no em dashes in any new copy; real photos only, never AI-generated machine images (Kim's hard rule); prices must match the locked figures (Clinic Starter Kit $49,995, 15D $29,995, 13D $19,995), don't invent or round differently across pages.

🎯First Light Civil, Lead-Gen & AI-Search Plan

Different intent to a HIFU clinic: people searching for civil or excavation work want a local, licensed, responsive contractor right now, not a long research journey. That means local pack (GBP) plus reviews plus fast-response signals matter more here than long-form content, at least at this stage.

Phase 1, Foundations (do first, roughly 1 to 2 weeks)

1Complete the GBP fully. It exists but is empty of trust signals. Add real photos (site work, machinery, before/after cuts, real only, no stock/AI images), services with descriptions (Excavation, Site Cuts & Pad Prep, Retaining Walls, Pool Excavation), the QBCC licence number in the description, service area (Brisbane + Moreton Bay suburbs), hours (already Mon-Sun 6am-6pm), and a proper category (Excavating contractor / Earthmoving contractor, check the current "Earth works company" is the best-matching Google category).
2Get the first 5 to 10 Google reviews. This is the single highest-leverage move for a trade business with zero reviews competing against contractors with 20+ years and established review counts. Ask every recent or current client directly (text/email after job completion) for a Google review, same "one channel, keep it simple" principle as Brisbane HIFU. A civil contractor with real reviews will out-rank a bigger competitor with none, in the local pack.
3On-page SEO basics. A unique title tag and meta description per page (currently unconfirmed and likely missing) mentioning "Brisbane" and "Moreton Bay" plus the specific service. Add LocalBusiness (or more specifically GeneralContractor/HomeAndConstructionBusiness) schema with the QBCC licence, service area, phone, and (once available) an aggregateRating.
4Citations, the exact same booking-agnostic pack used for Brisbane HIFU, in trade-appropriate versions. True Local, Yellow Pages, Hotfrog, Localsearch (all read heavily for trades), plus hipages and Oneflare (trade-specific lead marketplaces genuinely worth a free or low-cost listing here, unlike Fresha for HIFU, since the competitive-marketplace objection doesn't apply the same way to a trade with abundant demand), Bing Places (import from GBP), and Apple Business Connect. Identical NAP everywhere: business name, phone 0420 683 648, email, service area (no public street address needed if GBP is a service-area type).

Phase 2, Trust & content (weeks 2 to 6)

5A real project gallery. Photos of actual completed jobs (excavation, site cuts, retaining walls) with brief captions (suburb + scope). This is the single biggest credibility gap versus competitors, and it doubles as GBP photo content and social proof.
6Cost-guide content. The research found real search demand here ("how much do earthworks cost in Queensland", per-m³ rates). A simple, honest "What does excavation cost in Brisbane?" page (ranges, what affects price, soil type, access, scope) captures high-intent research traffic the way Brisbane HIFU's cost page does, and mirrors what ranking competitors (Civwil, MQC) already publish.
7Suburb-level pages, later. Once the core site, GBP and reviews are solid, add a small number of genuine (not templated or thin) location pages for the highest-value Moreton Bay/Brisbane growth suburbs, matching the "hand-tuned, self-canonical" approach already proven on the Brisbane HIFU suburb matrix.
8AI-search readiness. Once schema, reviews and citations are in place, run the same monthly rank-check: "excavation contractor Brisbane", "civil contractor near me Moreton Bay", "earthworks quote Brisbane", "licensed excavator Brisbane". Realistic expectation: local-pack and Google Maps visibility will move faster than the AI assistants, which need citation volume first, the same lag pattern seen with Brisbane HIFU on Gemini and Perplexity.
Why this is a fast win compared to Brisbane HIFU: the competitive bar for reviews and schema completeness in this space is genuinely low (the researched competitors mostly lean on tenure and fleet size, not digital polish). A licensed, responsive contractor with 10 solid Google reviews, a complete GBP, and basic schema can credibly out-rank far older competitors in the local pack within weeks, not months.
Guardrails: only claim what's verifiable. The QBCC licence number is real and should be used, but do not invent project counts, client numbers, or years of experience beyond what's already stated ("12+ years"). No em dashes in any new copy. Real project photos only.

🔁Shared Method (proven on Brisbane HIFU, 10 Jul 2026)

Both plans above reuse the exact playbook that got Brisbane HIFU Clinic named #1 by Google's AI answer on every tested "best HIFU Brisbane" prompt. The pattern, in order of leverage:

  1. Fix the lead pipe or booking path first. No SEO work matters if enquiries don't reach a human.
  2. Structured data (schema) so Google and AI assistants can read facts (services, prices, entity, reviews) directly, not guess from prose.
  3. Reviews on Google, specifically. The single highest-leverage trust and ranking signal, ask directly and keep it to one channel.
  4. Citations across the map and AI feeders that matter. Google Business Profile, Bing Places (feeds Copilot/ChatGPT), Apple Business Connect (feeds Siri/Apple Maps), plus category-relevant directories. Identical NAP everywhere.
  5. A monthly AI rank-check against fixed, realistic prompts, logging who gets named and why, so effort follows evidence instead of guesswork.
  6. Vet anything that asks for payment to "get you found" before paying. thebestbrisbane.com turned out to be a $989/yr pay-to-rank listing, not editorial. The same scepticism applies to any trade directory or "AI visibility" upsell either business encounters.
